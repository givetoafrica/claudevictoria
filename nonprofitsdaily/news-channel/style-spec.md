# Nonprofitsdaily News: format and visual spec

Built from format research of a faceless weekly news channel in another niche (public metadata, chapter list, frame
previews, and pacing only). Nothing here reuses that channel's script, thumbnails, voice, name or branding.
Rule (from the video-factory skill): format and pattern only, never content.

## Format (what we copy: the structure)
- Weekly roundup, 10 to 13 minutes, 6 to 8 chapters, each chapter named for its story and timestamped in the description.
- Cold open (about 40 s): the 3 to 4 biggest stories in one breath, strangest or most important first, then "let's get into it."
- Each story, same order every time: what happened -> who said it, with the number and the date -> why it matters -> Victoria's take.
- Sources are said out loud in the sentence, then shown on screen, then listed in the description ("Today's Sources", primary document first).
- Unconfirmed claims are labeled unconfirmed, out loud and on screen.
- A connecting thread near the end ties the stories together, then one question to the audience, then the sign-off.
- Description: chapters, "Today's Sources" with a link per story, then hashtags.

## Visual grammar (what we copy: how it looks)
- No presenter, no avatar, no stock footage. The picture is the source itself.
- The real page fills the frame in a browser-style window and scrolls slowly while the narrator reads.
- The key line is highlighted when the narrator says it, and the view zooms slightly toward it.
- Charts and data are shown directly, or rebuilt by us (credited) when the original is not ours to show.
- Short cutaways only when they carry meaning (a chart, a form, a table). No decoration.
- Our additions: a SOURCE chip (publisher, date) and a CONFIRMED / UNCONFIRMED tag on every source, a ticker, and a "Victoria's Take" card.

## Source rules (legal)
- US government pages (IRS, Treasury, Federal Register, Grants.gov, USAspending): show in full, public domain.
- Private publishers: headline plus one highlighted excerpt only, credited, with our own analysis. Never scroll a whole article.
- Never a logo, photo or chart from a private publisher as decoration.
- Every story has at least one primary source. Two sources for anything surprising.

## Tone (ours, not theirs)
Calm, mature, plain. No jokes at the subject's expense, no hype words. Say what is known, what is not, then give Victoria's take.

## Pacing targets
- Narration about 150 words per minute.
- A source stays on screen 8 to 20 s, then cuts to the next page or card. Scroll speed slow enough to read the highlighted line.
- A story is 90 to 180 s. The cold open is 30 to 45 s. The close is 30 to 45 s.
