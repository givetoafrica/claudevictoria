# Survival Untold: video rules (set by Victoria)

## Rule 1: every video gets a different setting
- Never make the video "two people talking in one room". An interview is the story, not the setting.
- Each video needs **at least 4 distinct settings**, with a visible screen change between them (iris wipe, push, zoom).
- Characters must be **moving through the world** in every setting: climbing stairs, chasing a car, riding a Ferris wheel, running across a rooftop, etc. No standing still while they talk.
- No setting is reused from a previous video. Check the list below before picking.
- Each setting has its own colour palette, props and one environment gag (something that reacts to the story).
- Hidden yellow duck in **every** setting (series signature). The last shot is the duck wearing a light bulb.

## Rule 2: what stays the same
- Script and voices are locked once Victoria approves them. Never reword, and keep every "MORON".
- Scout (red puffer jacket, ponytail, headphones, big reporter mic with the "SU" flag) is always big, in the foreground, always moving.
- Guest is a stickman in a suit with a big head, legs, expressive arms.
- Vox-explainer look: bold flat colours, thick outlines, white sticker outline on characters, halftone dots, sunburst.
- Highlighter captions at the top, a stamped insult word on each insult, lightning and thunder on the insults.
- Infographic card on each beat. Facts on cards must be real.
- Small AI disclosure on screen at the start and on the end card.
- Post at 10:00 AM PT, label "Shorts lane", pinned comment carries the meta description for discovery.

# The series: "GENIUSES GONE MAD" (formerly working-titled "Interviewing History's Biggest Egos")

Scout (deadpan intern, red puffer jacket, big "SU" mic) interviews a legendary ego. The moment she names their rival, the guest turns into an unstoppable yap machine: brilliant, petty, dramatic, personally offended. Always fictional comedy built on a real, documented rivalry.

## Episode shape (75-90 s target)
1. **Cold open:** Scout names the rival. Guest explodes with a long insult list.
2. **The real dispute:** two beats of true history (a fact card each).
3. **The roast:** Scout offers the rival's good side, guest rages.
4. **Guest gets exposed:** Scout brings up the guest's own documented flaws.
5. **Final meltdown:** "Who's the greater genius?" Guest builds to a boast and gets cut off.
6. **Power gag:** lights go out / the thing the guest bragged about fails. Scout gets the last line. Guest screams "GET! OUT!"
7. **End screen:** "WHO WON? X OR Y? COMMENT BELOW!" plus AI disclosure.

## Signature bits (every episode)
- The insult "MAGNIFICENT MORON".
- Hidden yellow duck in every setting. In the Tesla episode it hides behind a Tesla coil.
- A **calm pigeon** (or other bystander) watches the meltdown in every setting.
- **Every insult triggers an absurd visual** (stamped word + a doodle/gag): a portrait that catches a spark, a toaster that flies off a table, etc.
- Scout always gets the final line before the guest's last scream.

## Packaging
- Title: "<Guest> ROASTS <Rival> for 90 Seconds" (only when the runtime is close to 90 s; otherwise say the real length).
- Thumbnail text: guest's opening line in two short lines, e.g. "EDISON?! THAT MORON?!"
- Description: one-line hook, a "Historical accuracy" note (what is documented vs fictional), the AI disclosure, hashtags #Guest #Rival #GuestVsRival #HistoryComedy #FunnyHistory #YouTubeShorts.
- Pinned comment: duck question, the vote question, then the keyword meta description for discovery.
- YouTube playlist: "Interviewing History's Biggest Egos" (must be created on YouTube; the scheduling tool can't create playlists).

# Rules added with Episode 3 (Franklin at the airport)

## Clean screen
- **Don't put too much on the screen.** Captions plus at most ONE other graphic at a time. No stacked cards, doodles and stamps together. Space around the characters. Infographic cards are optional and only for a key fact.
- Facts must come out of the arguments, never from a lecture or a title card.

## Real Vox look
- Flat vector shapes, a limited palette per scene, soft shadows, a light paper-grain overlay, clean bold type.
- Smooth eased motion (anticipation and overshoot), parallax, small camera drift. No jittery filters.

## Camera language (every episode)
- Open on an **extreme-wide establishing shot**, then **medium-wide two-shots** for arguments, **close-ups** only for the strongest insults and deadpan punchlines, and back to wide whenever the guest causes physical chaos.
- Fast cuts, sudden zooms, dramatic pauses, sudden silence before punchlines.
- Keep the background alive (crowds reacting, things moving) and the space roomy.

## Comedy rules
- Hook inside the first second: the alarm/problem is already happening.
- Guest dominates with extreme yap; Scout is calm, dry, almost bored, and never competes on volume.
- A fresh joke, reaction or visual escalation every 5-7 seconds.
- Insults get more inventive, never repeat. Keep "MAGNIFICENT MORON" as the signature.
- Historical facts stay accurate; only the situation is outrageous.
- End on a physical gag plus a direct hook to the next episode.
- Hidden yellow duck, never acknowledged.
- Avoid labs, long intros, narration, and plain interview setups.

## Sound
- Real ambience for the setting, impact sounds, orchestral stings on big moments, and sudden silence before punchlines.

## Locations
- A brief can fix one location (like the airport). Then the video still uses at least 4 distinct zones of that place with cuts between them.

## Rules learned from Episode 3 (approved by Victoria)
- Keep the screen clean: captions plus one graphic at most. Victoria loved the uncluttered Ep3.
- Guest voice about 1.15x, never faster. Background sound stays very quiet (soft engine hum or room tone), no crowd noise. Sound effects sit under the voices.
- Legs must read as two separate legs: wide stance, visible knees, dark outlines. Walk and run cycles derive their phase from distance travelled so feet never slide.
- Cinematic camera: tracking moves, whip pans between locations, dutch angles on rants, depth-of-field blur on close-ups, film grain and a warm grade.
- A moving vehicle (car plus scooter) is a strong format: the scrolling background changes setting at each cut and the characters stay in motion.
- Ending pattern: guest gets angry, makes a physical exit (zoom away) and Scout delivers a deadpan hook to the next episode.

## Rules learned from Episode 4 (Wright brothers on a plane)
- Do not show characters walking. Victoria found the walk cycles awkward; the driving episode worked because legs were never seen.
- Hide the legs: use foreground blockers (seat backs, counters, panels) so characters are seen from the chest up, and change location with camera cuts and whip pans instead of walking.
- Moving vehicles beat static interiors. Victoria rated the plane-cabin setting about 65%, so prefer a vehicle or a setting with motion for the next episode.
- Two guests plus Scout works: give each guest a distinct voice (Orville higher, Wilbur gravelly) and a distinct cap and moustache.
- Episode 4 (Wright brothers) is scheduled for Sat Nov 14, 10:00 AM PT with an AUTO-WHAT?! thumbnail.

## House style change (approved by Victoria)
- New default look: paper cutout (South Park / Monty Python feel). Characters are flat pieces of paper with slightly wobbling torn edges that redraw every frame, motion stepped at about 12 fps, mouths that flap between open, half and shut, a warm cardboard tint and visible paper grain.
- It suits our no-walking rule because stiff sliding motion looks intentional. Keep legs hidden or minimal, and keep cutting between locations.
- Claymation was tested and rejected for now (soft and muddy at close range).
- The Vox flat-vector look stays as the fallback; Episodes 1-4 were made in it.
- The McCarthy AI episode (Ep5) is being rebuilt in the cutout style before scheduling.
